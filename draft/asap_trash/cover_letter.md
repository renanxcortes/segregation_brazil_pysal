| The Editors
| *Applied Spatial Analysis and Policy*

Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit the enclosed manuscript, **"Multidimensional Racial
Residential Segregation across Brazilian Cities: A Nationwide Assessment with 2022
Census Data,"** for consideration as a research article in *Applied Spatial
Analysis and Policy*.

**Context and importance.** Residential segregation by race is one of the most
durable axes of urban inequality in Brazil, conditioning access to services,
schooling, environmental quality and health. Yet almost all quantitative work on
the subject — nationwide and metropolitan alike — rests on a single measure, the
index of dissimilarity, and therefore on just one of the five dimensions of
segregation distinguished by Massey and Denton; spatially explicit measures are
rarely used, and most studies still draw on the 2010 Census or earlier. This
paper provides the first description of racial residential segregation in Brazil
that is at once **nationwide** (all 319 municipalities above 100,000 inhabitants),
**multidimensional** (nine indices spanning all five dimensions, five of them
spatially explicit), and based on the **2022 Census**, the most recent tract-level
data available. The analysis runs through a fully open and reproducible pipeline.

The results show that the choice of measure is consequential. The four evenness
indices are near-redundant, and on that dimension segregation is concentrated in
the South and Southeast — confirming and extending the reference 2010 study. But
the other dimensions do not follow: the exposure dimension inverts the regional
ranking, being highest in the North and Northeast for compositional reasons, and
the identity of "the most segregated city" changes entirely with the dimension
chosen (the dissimilarity and isolation rankings correlate at a Kendall's
τ of about −0.26). The Brazilian urban system varies along at least two, and
arguably three, independent axes. A one-index characterisation is therefore
incomplete, and — because the dimensions implicate different policy levers — the
measurement choice has practical stakes for how cities monitor and address
segregation.

**Fit with the journal.** The paper is an application of spatial analysis at
national scale with an explicit policy dimension, which is the core of this
journal's scope. It does not propose a new method; it uses established
spatially explicit indices to produce a spatially resolved, policy-relevant
description of an entire urban system, and it argues concretely for a
multidimensional monitoring dashboard and for region-specific policy responses
(service equalisation in the majority-minority North and Northeast; land-use and
housing instruments in the consolidated metropolitan cores of the South and
Southeast). Closely related work on the sensitivity of spatial segregation
indices and on comparative spatial segregation analytics has appeared in this
journal and in adjacent spatial-analysis venues, and the readership that uses
these indices is the audience this paper is written for. The reproducible
pipeline — package, driver scripts, pinned environment, and one-command
regeneration of every figure and table — reflects the journal's emphasis on
transparent, applied spatial analysis.

**Declarations.** The manuscript is original, has not been published previously,
and is not under consideration by any other journal. The uploaded manuscript is
anonymised for double-blind review. The study uses only publicly available data
aggregated by census tract, with no human participants, so ethics approval is not
applicable. The authors have no competing interests to declare, and no funding
was received for this work. Statistical inference is deliberately outside the
scope of the paper and is identified as future work; the contribution is a
point-estimation description.

Thank you for considering this submission. We look forward to the reviewers'
comments.
